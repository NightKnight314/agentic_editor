# Editorial method reference

Use this reference to select and operationalize an editing method. The full research note is at `../../../notes/editorial-methodologies.md`.

## Model the material

Index source time, transcript words and idea boundaries, speakers, shots/scenes, people, gaze, screen direction, action phase, object state, motion, image/audio quality, claims, chronology, rights, reactions, possible hooks/payoffs, and usable handles. Preserve low-confidence and unclassified moments; clean machine-legible footage is not automatically the best human moment.

Separate:

- **hard constraints:** valid media, rights/consent, sync, duration/delivery, truth, intelligibility;
- **soft objectives:** emotion, story, rhythm, performance, eye trace, continuity, novelty, relevance, retention, style;
- **warnings:** axis breaks, semantic jumps, weak handles, low-confidence reactions, lip mismatch, repeated imagery.

Generate globally coherent alternatives instead of greedily choosing the next highest-scoring shot.

## Method selection

### Continuity

Use for dialogue, demonstration, process, and factual explanation.

- Establish participant/location geometry before fragmenting it.
- Preserve the axis of action, eyelines, screen direction, object state, and compatible light/background.
- Cut on matching action when it carries attention across the seam.
- Use shot/reverse-shot selectively; a listener can carry more story than a speaker.
- Keep incoming attention near outgoing eye trace or guide the gaze with sound/motion/time.
- Treat the conventional 30-degree change as a jump-cut warning, not an absolute law.

### Expressive discontinuity

Use jump cuts, false matches, graphic matches, smash cuts, or ellipsis for compression, comedy, memory, tension, alienation, or conceptual association. Record an intended effect and estimated comprehension cost for every deliberate continuity violation.

### Documentary/interview

1. Transcribe and log complete context.
2. Build select reels by topic and character with handles.
3. Create a truthful paper/radio edit.
4. Test audio-only for clarity and cadence.
5. Add picture because it supplies evidence, place, contrast, emotion, or continuity.
6. Verify factual/ethical seams against source.

Protect hedges, negation, attribution, names, numbers, tense, chronology, and referents. Never use unrelated reaction, silence, or generic B-roll to imply guilt, hesitation, causality, or witnessed evidence.

### Dialogue/performance

- Cut ideas, intentions, reactions, breath, and power shifts—not just speaker turns.
- Preserve meaningful silence and overlap.
- Use J-cuts for anticipation and L-cuts for reaction/context while prior speech continues.
- Hide constructed speech only with legitimate coverage; protect lip sync and room tone.
- Check clipped phonemes, doubled consonants, breath/reverb truncation, and noise-floor pumping.

### B-roll

Rank by `literal evidence → specific illustration → context/atmosphere → generic filler`. Use it to prove, orient, show process/consequence, reveal character, bridge a truthful compression, or vary the visual field. Penalize clichés, incorrect context, repeated images, mismatched direction, and implied evidentiary status beyond provenance.

### Montage

Define purpose, start/end state, motif, progression, temporal model, audio spine, escalation, and payoff. Choose compression, rhythmic, tonal, intellectual, or parallel montage deliberately. Do not cut on every beat; preserve semantic boundaries and image/text reading time.

### Short-form

Open with a strong truthful result, question, contradiction, active moment, specific claim/evidence, or problem/promise. Match the opening to packaging, deliver value early, remove throat-clearing, and end after payoff. Treat retention curves as ambiguous evidence: spikes can be value or confusion; dips can be skipping or exit.

### Multicamera

Sync via verified timecode, markers/slate, waveform, distinctive event, then manual anchor. Store offset, drift, method, and confidence. Keep a stable program audio bed. Select angles for performance, active speaker, listener reaction, framing, continuity, and story. Use wide/two-shot fallback when speaker identity is ambiguous; avoid A/B metronome switching.

## Pacing controls

Pace is the rate of new information, emotion, action, and sensory change. Inspect shot-duration distribution and local trend, cut rate, beat duration, motion/luminance/audio curves, dialogue and pauses, semantic repetition, and setup-to-payoff time. Shape curves and contrast; do not impose a universal ideal shot length.

- Accelerate toward urgency when readability permits.
- Hold after payoff for absorption.
- Use duration and withholding for suspense.
- Time comedy as setup, turn, and reaction.
- Insert reset shots or silence after dense passages.
- Respect caption/text reading time.

## Review scales

Review at three levels:

1. **Seam/cut:** timing, handles, continuity, audio, sync, deception risk.
2. **Beat/scene:** changed state, prerequisites, progression, redundancy, coverage.
3. **Whole story:** central question, payoff, emotional curve, truth, required content, ending.

Key failure patterns: smooth but empty cuts, over/undercutting, noun-wallpaper B-roll, ping-pong dialogue, false documentary meaning, audio seams, retention cargo cults, montage without progression, missing handles, and applying high-energy style where testimony or comprehension needs time.
